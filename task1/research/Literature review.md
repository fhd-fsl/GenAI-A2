# Literature Review — Task 1: Research, Architecture Selection, and Transformer Fine-Tuning

This review covers five credible technical/scholarly sources relevant to **clinical eligibility reasoning**, **Transformer architectures for medical NLP**, **parameter-efficient supervised adaptation**, and **efficient inference foundations**. TrialGPT (the base framework for this assignment) is intentionally excluded; the selected papers provide complementary foundations for model selection, fine-tuning strategy, and inference considerations for Task 1.

| # | Paper Title | Venue / Year | Summary |
|---|-------------|--------------|---------|
| **1** | **Fine-Tuned Transformers and Large Language Models for Entity Recognition in Complex Eligibility Criteria for Clinical Trials** | *Kantor & Morzy, 2024* | Benchmarks BERT-based models (CODER, BioBERT, ClinicalBERT, BlueBERT, PubMedBERT, SciBERT) against GPT-4-turbo on clinical trial eligibility NER (15 entity classes, 120K annotations). **Key finding**: Domain-adapted BERT models (especially CODER, pre-trained on UMLS) **outperform GPT-4-turbo after moderate fine-tuning**, particularly in low-resource settings. Demonstrates that smaller, domain-aligned models remain competitive for structured clinical extraction tasks. Supports selection of a compact decoder fine-tuned on criterion-level eligibility. |
| **2** | **Optimal Strategies for Adapting Open-Source Large Language Models for Clinical Information Extraction: A Benchmarking Study in the Context of Ulcerative Colitis Research** | *Yim et al., medRxiv 2024* | Systematic benchmark of **QLoRA vs. full fine-tuning vs. prompting** on 6 open models (8–70B) for Mayo Endoscopic Subscore extraction from colonoscopy reports. **QLoRA improves accuracy by 8.3–15.6 pp** over base models; GPT-4o leads by only 2.5–5.4% at much higher cost. Provides direct evidence that **4-bit QLoRA on 7–8B models is the Pareto-optimal choice** for clinical IE on constrained hardware. Validates QLoRA as the fine-tuning method for Task 1. |
| **3** | **High-Fidelity Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning for Joint Recognition and Linking of Diagnoses to ICD-10 in Non-Standard Primary Care Notes** | *Estupiñán-Ojeda et al., JAMIA Open 2025* | Compares **LoRA, QLoRA, DoRA, LoHA, LoKR** on multilingual BERT/RoBERTa/mDeBERTa for joint entity recognition + ICD-10 linking (21K bilingual clinical notes). **QLoRA achieves 62.2 Micro-F1 vs. FFT 63.0** (1.3% gap) with **67.5% fewer trainable params and 33.7% less memory**. Rank sensitivity analysis shows **r ≥ 128 is critical for token-level tasks**; lower ranks sharply degrade F1. Validates QLoRA with sufficient rank for evidence-span tasks like criterion-level eligibility with sentence-ID evidence. |
| **4** | **A Compact 3B Language Model Achieving Competitive MedQA Performance with Minimal Supervision** | *Subaharan, TechRxiv 2025* | Fine-tunes **Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct** with CoT-SFT + QLoRA on 19.7K medical reasoning samples. Achieves **47.8% on MedQA**, surpassing PMC-Llama-7B (45.6%) and HEAL-13B (47.2%). Demonstrates that **sub-7B models can approach specialized 7B medical LLMs** with modest supervised fine-tuning. Supports selection of a 3–4B target model for Task 1 given hardware constraints. |
| **5** | **Prompt Sensitivity and Answer Consistency in Small Open-Source Language Models for Clinical Question Answering** | *Hariprasad, arXiv 2026 (2603.00917)* | Evaluates **Llama-3.2-3B, Phi-3-Mini-3.8B, Gemma-2-2B, Mistral-7B, Meditron-7B** on MedQA, MedMCQA, PubMedQA with 5 prompt styles (15K inferences). **Llama-3.2-3B achieves highest accuracy (49–65%) with best consistency/accuracy balance**. Gemma-2-2B shows high consistency (0.86) but low accuracy (33–43%) — "reliably incorrect." Phi-3-Mini-3.8B: 48–53% accuracy but high UNKNOWN rate (10.5%). **Llama-3.2-3B recommended for low-resource clinical deployment**. Provides empirical basis for 3B-class model viability. |

## Synthesis for Task 1

| Task 1 Requirement | Key Takeaways from Literature |
|--------------------|------------------------------|
| **Clinical eligibility reasoning** | Domain-adapted models (CODER, BioMedical, MedMobile) outperform general large models on clinical criterion tasks (Sources 1, 3, 4). Criterion-level classification + evidence extraction maps to token-level NER/span tasks (Source 3). |
| **Transformer architecture** | Decoder-only LLMs (Llama, Qwen, Phi, Mistral) with GQA reduce KV cache; 3–8B parameter range balances capacity and deployability (Sources 4, 5). |
| **Supervised adaptation (fine-tuning)** | **QLoRA (4-bit NF4, r=128–256)** matches LoRA within 2–4 pp while fitting 6–8 GB GPUs (Sources 2, 3). Rank ≥128 required for token-level evidence tasks (Source 3). CoT-SFT boosts medical reasoning in small models (Source 4). |
| **Efficient inference (foundations)** | KV caching, GQA, and quantization are standard for consumer GPU deployment (Sources 4, 5). Speculative decoding (Task 5) requires tokenizer-compatible draft model — noted for model-pair selection. |
| **Model scale for 6 GB GPU** | 3–4B models fit comfortably with headroom; 7–8B models fit at Q4_K_M with 4–6K context (Sources 4, 5). Qwen3-4B and Llama-3.2-3B have identical-tokenizer draft pairs for Task 5. |
